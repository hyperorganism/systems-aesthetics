# Systems Aesthetics

Art, artificial life and systems practice. How do artworks use processes, autonomy, emergence and living materials, and how do those practices change participation, authorship and interpretation? Artwork dates identify the work or the start of a series; linked documentation may be later.

## Critical foundations

- **Systems esthetics** - Burnham frames artworks through processes, environments and relationships among components, including people. A critical account of artistic practice, with Haacke and other process-based works as examples. [Burnham 1968](<https://www.artforum.com/features/systems-esthetics-201372/> "Systems Esthetics; Artforum").
- **Artificial-life art** - Examines artificial evolution, ecologies, robots and pattern-generating systems as artistic practices. Connects technical processes to questions of emergence, agency and artistic authorship. [Whitelaw 2004](<https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262731768/metacreation/> "Metacreation: Art and Artificial Life; MIT Press (book)").

## Process, environment and interaction

- **CYSP 1 (1956)** - Schoffer's cybernetic sculpture uses sensors and motors to respond to its surroundings. Environmental signals enter the movement of the artwork rather than serving only as background. [Schöffer 1956](<https://www.dacs.org.uk/artwork/cysp-1-spatiodynamic-cybernetics-1956-65170> "CYSP 1; DACS artwork record").
- **Condensation Cube (1965)** - Water evaporates and condenses inside an acrylic enclosure, producing patterns that change with ambient conditions. The MACBA work makes environmental exchange and material process visible. [Haacke 1965](<https://fundaciomacba.es/en/obres/condensation-cube/> "Condensation Cube; MACBA collection record").
- **Colloquy of Mobiles (1968)** - Suspended machines interact through light and sound; visitors can interrupt or join their exchanges. Documentation of the 2018 reconstruction distinguishes the 1968 installation from its rebuilt control hardware and software. [Pask 1968](<https://www.colloquyofmobiles.com/> "Colloquy of Mobiles; COLLOQUY 2018 reconstruction documentation").

## Evolution and artificial ecologies

- **A-Volve (1994)** - Visitors draw bodies that become simulated creatures in an interactive pool. Shape affects movement and survival; predation, reproduction and visitors' gestures alter the artificial ecology. [Sommerer and Mignonneau 1994](<https://www.ntticc.or.jp/en/archive/works/a-volve/> "A-Volve; NTT InterCommunication Center").
- **Galapagos (1997)** - Visitors stand before preferred animated forms to select their survivors and offspring. Human taste supplies selection pressure while mutation and recombination explore the encoded possibilities. [Sims 1997](<https://www.karlsims.com/galapagos/index.html> "Galápagos; Artist documentation").
- **Picbreeder** - Users select images and branch one another's evolutionary lineages. Collaborative selection explores different aesthetic directions while the underlying neural representation can increase in complexity. [Secretan et al. 2008](<https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357328> "Picbreeder: Evolving Pictures Collaboratively Online; CHI").
- **After ALife Ahead (2017)** - An installation couples living organisms, cultured cells, sensing and architectural changes in a former ice rink. The exhibition constructs an interdependent environment whose processes unfold over time. [Huyghe 2017](<https://www.skulptur-projekte-archiv.de/en-us/2017/projects/186/> "After ALife Ahead; Skulptur Projekte archive").

## Bodies and living materials

- **Strandbeest (since 1990)** - Wind-powered sculptures use leg linkages; some store compressed air or sense water mechanically. Jansen's demonstrations show designed adaptations, with continued assistance from their maker. [Jansen 1990](<https://www.kunstmuseum.nl/en/exhibitions/theo-jansen> "Strandbeests: The New Generation; Kunstmuseum Den Haag (2022 exhibition documentation)"); [video](<https://www.ted.com/talks/theo_jansen_my_creations_a_new_form_of_life>), [project](<https://www.strandbeest.com/>).
- **Semi-living Worry Dolls (2000)** - Cultured cells grow on shaped polymer scaffolds maintained in an exhibition setting. The work makes tissue outside an organism and its dependence on human care visible. First shown at Ars Electronica in 2000. [Catts et al. 2000](<https://tcaproject.net/portfolio/worry-dolls/> "Semi-living Worry Dolls; Artist documentation (first exhibition 2000)").

## Open questions

- What is delegated to the system, what remains controlled by its makers, and how can participants perceive that division?
- How do maintenance, reconstruction and changing materials affect the identity and interpretation of a work?
